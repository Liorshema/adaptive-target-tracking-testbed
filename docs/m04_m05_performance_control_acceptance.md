The controller runs at 20 Hz simulated time; actuation runs at 50 Hz. Computation
duration is measured by `perf_counter` around the full control pipeline, including
safety, and excludes ROS command/telemetry publication. It is wall time, not solver
CPU time or complete callback latency. Maxima come from retained successful solves;
failed/invalid cycles have no equivalent timing samples. Comparing to 50 ms is a
nominal-period reference, not a measured hard real-time deadline-miss count; actual
wall spacing depends on RTF and scheduling. Historical observer return codes were
1 for both captures because the physical-speed gate failed, not a launch crash.

Inactive durations hold each observed telemetry flag until the next stamp, clipping
to the settled window; they are sampled estimates, not physics-step certification.
Control uses approximately 20 Hz telemetry; actuation diagnostics use 10 Hz. The
aggressive control hold was `[38.804,40.850]` sim s, reason `Target prediction is
stale`. Actuator holds also include position recovery and transport rejection.
Both captures eventually resume; no permanent stop was observed. Recovery counter
maxima do not prove every brief transition was captured. Whole-run startup waiting
events are retained in the original recordings and excluded only by the stated
settling policy, not deleted.

### Estimation, prediction and freshness

M03's 30 ms holdback plus 100 Hz draining preserves observation stamps. Both runs
have fusion-age p95 40 ms; rare maxima are 200 ms nominal and 138 ms aggressive.
There are no backward commits or fusion failures. Nominal joints reject 1/23,380;
aggressive 2/24,540 (future stamps). Wheel late rejection is 3/3,302 and 2/3,391.
Four pending entries at shutdown in each run are not rejected observations.

The controller allows robot-state age 0.2 s and prediction age 0.5 s. Predictor
output carries the source target-state reference stamp, not publication time; its
20 samples use 0.1 s steps. Whole-capture observer counts divided by configured
60 s give approximate robot-estimate / prediction rates 42.02 / 17.53 Hz nominal
and 42.63 / 16.47 Hz aggressive. These counts include startup receipts and are not
strictly settled-window rate estimates. Predictor/target-estimator callbacks were
not individually timed in these artifacts: no claim of their computation-time
distribution or exact prediction receipt-age distribution is available.

Aggressive stale-prediction rejection shows that freshness is not uninterrupted.
Recorded 41 settled control rejections cause a stop/hold instead of continuing on
stale input. The original full-capture blocked counts also include 46 waiting-target
and 9 observer feedback-skew events. Observer skew is an evaluation omission, not
proof of a controller safety failure. Actuator final sampled counters include 27
command rejections in the aggressive run; they are not 27 distinct physical fault
events. The original telemetry retains reasons and ordering evidence.

M03 residual mean base position/yaw errors are 0.069516 m / 6.038 deg nominal and
0.180886 m / 12.666 deg aggressive. The controlled drive established skid-turn
odometry mismatch despite correct wheel dimensions, signs and initial-frame
alignment. Joint covariance remains overconfident during aggressive acceleration.
Those findings are preserved in [`m03_estimation_localization.md`](m03_estimation_localization.md).

These are process-tree samples taken roughly once per wall second, with warm-up
samples excluded. They are neither system-wide CPU accounting nor a causal
attribution of each callback. The external observer/native-pose capture adds load
outside this accounting. RSS/PSS, memory peaks, leak trends, per-stage predictor CPU
timing and a trace-free deployment RTF were **not captured** and remain unavailable.
No current idle memory snapshot is substituted for historical runtime measurements.

The largest observed CPU users are estimation and actuation; the workload is spread
over multiple Python/ROS workers and Gazebo. Controller mean/p95 fits its nominal
period but has occasional longer wall times. Aggressive prediction availability is
a larger acceptance concern than WLS solver micro-optimization. A historical
profile (`validation/aggressive_A/control_profile.txt`, earlier source revision)
spent about 0.178/0.982 s in WLS over 40 solves; it supports avoiding a speculative
solver rewrite but is not a current-source benchmark.

**No additional performance optimization is justified in this task.** Existing M03
closed-form Q already reduced its isolated covariance computation cost from about
275 to 35 us. Existing headless/no-marker operation avoids optional visualization
load. Further worker consolidation, sensor throttling, covariance shortcuts,
physics changes or WSL graphics investigation would exceed the evidence and scope.
RTF meets the existing 0.5 test floor but is below real-time 1.0; no new threshold
was invented or relaxed.
